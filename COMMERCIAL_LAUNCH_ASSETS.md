## PART 3 — 7-DAY LINKEDIN CONTENT CALENDAR

### Day 1 (Monday) — Authority Positioning
**Hook:** "Most B2B SaaS founders think their conversion problem is a traffic problem. It's not."

**Body:**
Traffic is a distribution problem. Conversion is a cognition problem.

The distinction matters because they require completely different interventions.

A traffic problem: you need more of the right people arriving.
A conversion problem: you need the right people who arrive to complete the journey.

Here's what a conversion problem actually looks like:
- Bounce rate is high despite low bounce keywords
- Form start rate is above 40% but completion is below 15%
- Session time is above 2 minutes but conversion is below 1%
- Retargeting converts at 3× the rate of cold traffic (intent exists, friction kills it)

If any of those patterns appear in your analytics, you don't have a traffic problem.

You have a friction problem.

And friction has a specific anatomy.

**CTA:** What does your form completion rate look like? Drop a number below — I'll tell you if it's a friction signal.

---

### Day 2 (Tuesday) — Mechanism Education
**Hook:** "The 4 friction types that kill B2B SaaS conversion. None of them appear in Google Analytics."

**Body:**
Conversion friction is invisible in standard analytics. You see symptoms (bounce rate, exit rate), never causes.

The 4 types I diagnose:

1. **Cognitive Load Friction** — Too many decisions too fast. The user's working memory exceeds capacity. They pause. They leave. You see it as "abandoned session."

2. **Trust Deficit Friction** — The gap between your claim and your proof. Not lying — just insufficient evidence at the moment of skepticism. Form start high, submission low.

3. **Value Clarity Friction** — The user isn't sure the outcome applies to them. Generic copy. Weak specificity. "Results may vary" energy.

4. **Momentum Break Friction** — A single UX discontinuity (unexpected redirect, slow load, form validation error) that breaks the behavioral flow. The user doesn't know why they stopped.

Each type has a distinct signature in behavioral data.

Each requires a different fix.

**CTA:** Which one sounds most familiar in your funnel?

The observation that matters: friction lives where attention is high and trust is still being formed.

That's almost never where you're looking.
