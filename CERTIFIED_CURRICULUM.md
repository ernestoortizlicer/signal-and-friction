# 3. CASE STUDY LIBRARY PLAN — 5 OUTLINES

## Case 1 — "The Phantom Conversion": SaaS Onboarding Collapse, India Market
**Industry:** B2C SaaS (enterprise edition with regional teams)
**Funnel Stage:** Post-signup activation → feature adoption → day-7 retention
**Stated Problem:** "Our localized Indian version has 45% lower day-7 retention than our US version despite identical features."

**What the Data Showed:**
- Day-7 retention: US 61%, India 34%
- SMS OTP delivery rate: US 99.2%, India 71.4%
- PostHog funnel: 34% of Indian users never completed OTP step (US: 2%)
- Session recordings: users waiting 45–90 seconds for OTP — then abandoning

**The Friction (Isolated Finding):**
SMS gateway routing through a US carrier caused 28-second average delay → 90-second P99 latency for Indian mobile numbers. The onboarding flow had a 30-second OTP timeout with no resend prompt.

**Intervention (One Change):**
Gateway vendor changed to India-native Exotel. OTP timeout extended to 120 seconds. Resend prompt added at 60 seconds.

**Telemetry Verification (30 days post):**
- OTP completion: 71% → 94%
- Day-7 retention India: 34% → 58%
- Revenue impact: +$280K ARR from Indian cohort

**Diagnostic Lesson:** Localization failures are rarely content problems. They are infrastructure problems disguised as cultural problems.

---

## Case 2 — "The Invisible Paywall": Enterprise Trial Conversion Collapse
**Industry:** B2B SaaS (design tool, enterprise tier)
**Funnel Stage:** Trial → enterprise plan conversion
**Stated Problem:** "We have 40% trial activation but only 3% trial-to-enterprise conversion. We've rewritten the pricing page 3 times."

**What the Data Showed:**
- 40% of trial users hit the paywall feature (live collaboration)
- Of those, 82% viewed the pricing page
- Pricing page exit rate: 89%
- Session recordings: Users scrolling past 5 pricing tiers, spending <8 seconds on the enterprise option

**The Friction (Isolated Finding):**
Cognitive overload from 5-tier pricing architecture. Enterprise decision-makers (VP-level) encountered 5 options but had no authority signal — no enterprise-specific entry point, no "talk to sales" CTA above the fold. They were being asked to self-select into a $50K+ contract from a generic pricing page.

**Intervention (One Change):**
Enterprise tier removed from public pricing page entirely. Enterprise visitors (detected by company email domain) routed to a dedicated landing page with a single CTA: "Get Enterprise Pricing for [Company Name]" (personalized via Clearbit).

**Telemetry Verification (45 days post):**
- Trial-to-enterprise inquiry rate: 3% → 11%
- Sales team reported 60% increase in qualified inbound
- Enterprise ACV unchanged (pricing discipline preserved)

**Diagnostic Lesson:** The most expensive tier should never compete for attention with the cheapest tier. Pricing architecture is a cognitive architecture.

---

## Case 3 — "The Telemetry Opt-Out Cascade": Developer Tool Activation Drop
**Industry:** B2B SaaS (developer infrastructure, Vercel-type platform)
**Funnel Stage:** Account creation → first deployment → telemetry opt-in
**Stated Problem:** "We added telemetry for product improvements but 30-day activation dropped 22% the week after launch."

**What the Data Showed:**
- Telemetry opt-in modal appeared on first login, full-screen, blocking dashboard
- Opt-in rate: 18% (industry average: 42%)
- PostHog: 31% of users closed the app entirely within 30 seconds of seeing the modal
- Heatmap: 0% of users clicked "Learn More" — 82% clicked "Decline" without reading

**The Friction (Isolated Finding):**
GDPR-compliance anxiety triggered by modal design: white background (medical/legal aesthetic), 4-paragraph legal text, full-screen blocking (no "skip for now" option). Developer audience pattern-matched to cookie consent modals — triggered automatic rejection behavior without reading.

**Intervention (One Change):**
Modal redesigned as a non-blocking bottom toast: "Help us improve [Product] — takes 5 seconds." Opt-in/Decline below. Legal details linked, not displayed. Appeared on third login (not first).

**Telemetry Verification (60 days post):**
- Opt-in rate: 18% → 54%
- 30-day activation returned to baseline
- Legal team approved the toast design as GDPR-compliant

**Diagnostic Lesson:** Compliance is not an excuse for bad UX. Every compliance requirement has a UX-literate implementation.

## Case 4 — "The $50M Asia Expansion Freeze": Multi-Market SaaS Collapse
**Industry:** B2B SaaS (mid-market, CRM-adjacent)
**Funnel Stage:** Market expansion → localized onboarding → enterprise sales cycle
**Stated Problem:** "We expanded to Japan, Singapore, and South Korea simultaneously. 18 months in, MRR is $280K vs. projected $1.2M. Board is asking why."

**What the Data Showed:**
- Japan MRR: $40K (target: $400K) — sales cycle: 14 months average
- Singapore MRR: $180K (target: $500K) — churn at 90 days post-renewal
- South Korea MRR: $60K (target: $300K) — product not localized, sales team US-based
- Support ticket themes: Japan (formal approval processes incompatible with SaaS billing cycles), Singapore (data sovereignty), South Korea (language)

**The Friction (Isolated Finding, per market):**
Not one problem — three distinct friction architectures:
- **Japan:** Annual billing requirement (Japanese enterprises cannot approve monthly SaaS subscriptions at VP level — requires board-level vendor approval for anything not annual). The SaaS had monthly billing only.
- **Singapore:** Financial sector clients (42% of pipeline) required ISO 27001 certification. Product had SOC 2 only. Sales team was not aware.
- **South Korea:** Product interface entirely in English. Support in English only. Sales reps in US timezone.

**Intervention (per market, sequenced):**
1. Japan: Annual billing tier added. Japanese sales motion redesigned around vendor approval cycle.
2. Singapore: ISO 27001 certification fast-tracked (6-month sprint). Pipeline paused and re-engaged post-certification.
3. South Korea: Local reseller partner engaged. UI localization contracted.

**Telemetry Verification (12 months post):**
- Japan MRR: $40K → $220K
- Singapore pipeline velocity: 14 months → 7 months
- South Korea: reseller active, MRR $40K → $95K

**Diagnostic Lesson:** "APAC expansion" is not one problem. It is 3 sovereign markets with 3 different compliance architectures, 3 procurement cultures, and 3 language requirements. Treating it as one initiative is the friction.

## Case 5 — "The Pricing Anchor Collapse": High-Ticket B2B Checkout
**Industry:** Professional Services SaaS (legal tech)
**Funnel Stage:** Proposal → contract signing → payment
**Stated Problem:** "We raised our enterprise price 40% in January. Conversion dropped 55%. We can't tell if it's the price or the way we're presenting it."

**What the Data Showed:**
- Pre-raise: $28K ACV, 34% proposal-to-close rate
- Post-raise: $39K ACV, 15% proposal-to-close rate
- Win/loss interviews (10 deals): 8 of 10 said "the price seemed high for what we understood we were getting"
- Proposal document: PDF, 12 pages, price revealed on page 11
- No ROI calculation in proposal. No benchmark comparison. No deconstruction of value.

**The Friction (Isolated Finding):**
Price anchoring failure. The $39K number landed cold — no comparator, no ROI framing, no deconstruction. Buyers were comparing it to the previous price they'd heard in the market ($28K) and to generic SaaS tools ($500–$3K/month). The proposal was selling features, not value-in-dollars.

**Intervention (One Change):**
Proposal restructured: Page 1 opens with ROI calculation specific to client's stated problem ("Based on your 200-seat team and 40% manual review workload, this platform eliminates $82,000 in annual paralegal hours. Investment: $39,000."). Price introduced as line 3 of page 1, not page 11.

**Telemetry Verification (90 days post):**
- Proposal-to-close: 15% → 29%
- No discount requests in any of the 9 closed deals (previously: 70% of deals included discount negotiation)
- ACV maintained at $39K

**Diagnostic Lesson:** Price objections are almost always value-framing failures. The price is not the friction — the absence of an ROI anchor is.

# 4. CERTIFICATION EXAM BLUEPRINT

## Philosophy
*No multiple-choice. No theory. Real work.*

The S&F Certified™ exam is not a knowledge test. It is a performance test. The examiner does not care what the practitioner knows — only what they can produce, under time constraint, at the standard clients pay $4,500 to access.

The exam proves one thing: **can this person conduct a Signal & Friction diagnostic that Ernesto would sign his name to?**

---

## Exam Structure

**Format:** Live Diagnostic Simulation
**Window:** 72 hours from access grant
**Access:** Encrypted exam brief sent to practitioner's registered email at T=0
**Submission:** Deliverable uploaded to S&F certified portal by T+72h
**Review Period:** 5 business days
**Result:** Pass / Remediation Required / Fail

---

## Exam Brief Structure
Practitioners receive:
1. **Client Background** (2 paragraphs — company type, team size, product)
2. **Stated Problem** (1 paragraph — what the client says is wrong)
3. **Funnel Data Package** (PostHog export CSV + 3 funnel screenshots + 2 session recording descriptions)
4. **Available Context** (support ticket themes, NPS comments excerpt, 3 competitor comparison quotes)
5. **Scope Constraint:** "You have 72 hours. You are delivering to the CEO. No call will occur."

---

## Required Deliverable
Practitioners must produce:

**Document 1 — The Diagnosis (max 600 words)**
- Primary Friction Point identified and named with clinical precision
- Evidence chain (which data points, in what combination, produced this conclusion)
- What was considered and ruled out (the exclusion list — demonstrates rigor)
- Confidence level (and what additional data would change it)

**Document 2 — The Intervention Brief (max 400 words)**
- Single recommended change (not a list — one thing)
- Implementation level (copy change / UX change / technical change / process change)
- Estimated effort (hours of developer/designer time)
- Success metric: what PostHog event should move, in what direction, by what magnitude, in 30 days

**Document 3 — The Executive Cover (max 200 words)**
- Self-contained summary written for a CEO who will read only this
- Must contain: finding, intervention, expected outcome, confidence level
- Must not contain: jargon, qualifications, hedges, or requests for a call

---

## Grading Rubric (100 points)

| Criterion | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Isolation Quality | 30 pts | Did they find the right thing? Is the friction point causal, not correlational? |
| Evidence Chain | 20 pts | Is the conclusion derived from data, not intuition? Are alternative explanations excluded? |
| Intervention Specificity | 20 pts | Is the recommendation actionable by a developer in one sprint without further input? |
| Executive Clarity | 20 pts | Could a CEO act on this without a call? No jargon, no hedges, no "it depends"? |
| Scoping Discipline | 10 pts | Did they stay focused? Zero bonus points for mentioning additional findings. |

**Pass Threshold:** 75/100
**Remediation:** Practitioners scoring 60–74 may resubmit once with a different case brief.
**Fail:** Below 60 — must retake from Module 2.

---

## Exam Pools
S&F maintains 12 distinct exam briefs across 4 industry verticals (SaaS onboarding, enterprise B2B, marketplace/checkout, developer tools). Each practitioner receives one randomly. Briefs rotate annually to prevent leakage.
